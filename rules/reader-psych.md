# Reader psychology (short external prose)

After structure and register are clean, run three questions on the whole piece. Fail any one → rewrite even if no banned words remain.

## 1. Safe?

Does this feel like a pitch, a trap, or a sequence working a lead?

- De-threaten with real identity and one honest reason.  
- Do **not** use "I'm not selling" (that line is itself a tell).  
- One clear intent beats a long soft preamble.

## 2. Fluent?

Can a busy person get it in **one read** without translating abstract language into their week?

- Prefer near-mode words: people, money, time, what broke, what you need.  
- If the reader has to decode your thesis into their Tuesday, rewrite.

## 3. Ego-true?

Is there a clear way saying yes feels useful for *them* (expert, helper, peer) rather than a favor to a stranger with a thesis?

- Advice-asks beat opinion-asks.  
- Do not pre-insult the meeting ("dumb questions," "half-baked").  
- Experts like to sharpen something real.

## Cost of yes

- One small ask beats dual CTAs.  
- A reply that can succeed in two lines lowers friction.  
- Long agendas and dual options raise it.

## Scope

Use this check hard for: emails, DMs, cold notes, short asks, landing blurbs.

For long essays, still prefer clarity and one reader in mind; the three questions matter most when attention is scarce.
