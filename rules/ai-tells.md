# AI tells — audit checklist

Run the draft against these. Quote the offender, name the tell, fix it. Structural first; vocabulary last.

## Structural AI tells

1. **Uniform sentence length** — even 15–25 word metronome. Fix: shatter the meter.
2. **Rule-of-three tricolon** — everything in threes. Fix: two, four, or a sentence.
3. **Negative parallelism** — "not just X, it's Y." Fix: state the claim.
4. **Signpost framing** — "Here's the thing," "What stuck with me." Fix: lead with the thing.
4b. **Interest-bridge / interest-label (kill on sight)** — "That's where it gets interesting," "That makes it weirdly interesting," "Here's where it gets real," "the part that actually matters." Fix: delete the label; keep only the claim or scene.
4c. **"The part / section / bit X" pointer (kill on sight)** — "the part people see," "the section that matters," "the bit where he left it," "the part he saved for last." Fix: name the place or action without the meta-pointer.
4d. **Definitional punch (kill on sight)** — short snap after a metaphor: "That joining is fusion." "This is gravity." Fix: name it inside a full ordinary sentence.
4e. **Fake-plain gloss** — "shows up as heat and light," "daylight is what falls out," "comes out as energy." Fix: ordinary verbs (becomes, is released as, you get).
5. **Hollow aphoristic closer** — "In the end, the data doesn't lie." Fix: end on a detail.
5b. **False humility closer** — "No deep lesson here" / "Maybe no big takeaway" then a tidy moral. Fix: cut the bow; end on the sharpest unfinished point.
6. **Appositive piling** — stacked renames of the same noun. Fix: one or none.
7. **Over-smooth transitions** — Moreover / Furthermore / Additionally padding.
8. **Every paragraph same shape** — claim, elaborate, hedge, upbeat resolve.
9. **Hedged-to-center neutrality** — never takes a side.
10. **Reassurance pair** — two tidy sentences pre-rebutting an objection.
11. **Self-posed rhetorical question** — "The result? A complete rethink."
12. **False range** — "from innovation to cultural transformation."
13. **Fractal summary** — intro previews, body says, conclusion retells.
14. **Missing mess** — zero asides, zero tonal breaks, frictionless glass.
15. **Grandiose stakes inflation** — "fundamentally reshape how organizations…"
16. **Listicle in a trench coat** — "First… Second… Third…" disguised as prose.

## Performance tells (over-correction)

Trying too hard to sound human:

- **P1** Constructed antithesis for show  
- **P2** Telegraphic fragment-stacking ("Brutal. Labor thinning. Floor by 2026.")  
- **P3** Inserted quirky aside (charm bait)  
- **P4** One-word dramatic sentence standing alone  
- **P5** Mannered aphorism / kicker closer  
- **P6** Punchy-for-effect phrasing that a normal person wouldn't type  
- **P7** Default short declarative openers — bare 4–8 word S–V–O hooks as paragraph or piece openers ("The mower started on the third pull." "He cut the front in straight lines."). Fix: hitch in the same sentence, start mid-action, lead with mess, or fold into a longer line. Cap density; never stack as consecutive openers.
- **P8** Over-clever personification / workshop metaphor (soft tell, not a rotating banlist) — lines no one would say: lawns "winning," property lines as jokes "aimed at" someone, nuclei having "brief meetings," a force that "wins every time." Fix: plain what happened. Do not build a catalog of banned metaphors; flag and cut when it smells like an exercise.
- **P9** Weird verb-as-noun reification (soft tell) — "building and holding that squeeze," "the shove," "the joining" as a cute object. Fix: use a normal verb or a standard noun (pressure, collision, fusion).
- **P10** Faux-casual science / explainers (soft tell) — "hydrogen's kind," "not kitchen chemistry," "not tabletop physics." Fix: ordinary wording (hydrogen nuclei; hard to do on Earth).
- **P11** Meta teach-back cosplay (soft tell, often hard-kill in explainers) — "If that still feels thin…," "the usual gaps are…," "if you try teaching it back without notes…" Fix: drop the method commentary; keep the explanation.

**Rule:** if a line sounds like a copywriting class, be suspicious.

## Product / content tells (modern agents pass banlists)

- **Generic-vivid detail:** swap-out "specifics" (pasta sauce jars, forty yards, twelve feet closer). Prefer user grit or plain speech.
- **Stock internet bits** in observational prose: cart-return morality test, parking territoriality, slow driver hunting a closer spot — only if the user actually cares; never invent as flavor.
- **Simulated first-person without cost:** "I have definitely had moments where…" with no private fact. Use their moments or stay second/third person carefully.
- **Sealed observational kit:** hook → contrast → 2–4 vignettes → role-flip → soft sociology close. Break the kit; leave a loop open.
- **Multi-beat sealed assist** (email/chat): reframe + expand + redefine + resource pitch + justify. Send the one useful point.

## Register tells (short external: email, DM, short ask)

17. Soft padded setup ("I've been trying to understand how…")  
18. High-construal abstraction the reader must translate into their week  
19. Essayist stuck-point staging before the real question  
20. Mini scene-setting in cold short prose  
21. Writerly compounds / soft metaphors where plain words work  
22. Performed humility ("I'd value your perspective")  
23. Dual-option CTA (engineered off-ramp)  
24. Dual asks in a row  
25. Template soft-out cadence ("Totally understand if you're swamped")  
26. Unfinished intimacy (personal detail with no payoff)  
27. Over-deprecation ("half-baked takes") that undercuts the gift of asking  
28. Uniform smooth register end-to-end — no ordinary word  

**Meta:** a draft can have zero banned words and still read AI. Re-read as if you typed it on your phone to a real person.

## Email / short-message anti-templates

Phrases that could appear in 10,000 cold or polite emails — rewrite the actual thought:

- "Hope this finds you well"  
- "I came across [your X]" / "I'm reaching out because"  
- "I noticed [Company] recently…" / "caught my attention" / "caught my eye"  
- "I'd love to" as opener  
- "I'm passionate about" / "thrilled to"  
- "from your seat" / "from where you sit"  
- "I'm not trying to sell you" / "this isn't commercial"  
- "resonate" / "shaped how I think"  
- "what that actually looks like in practice"  
- "the real bottleneck" (and other "the real X" intensifiers)  
- Business meme clichés: "move the needle," "low-hanging fruit," "circle back," "double-click," "skin in the game"  

**Meta-rule:** if it sounds selected from "professional but warm," it is AI. Plain words for the real thought.

**Second meta-rule:** do not recite the recipient's bio back at them. Personalization is *your* honest reason for writing, not their LinkedIn read aloud.

## Vocabulary smell list (rotates; not the foundation)

Avoid as empty AI defaults: delve, tapestry, testament, landscape (abstract), realm, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, leverage (for "use"), harness, navigate (metaphor), foster, empower, elevate, unleash, ecosystem (abstract), journey (abstract), paradigm, pivotal, underscore(s), highlight (verb padding), meticulous, holistic, synergy, utilize, game-changer, nestled, vibrant, thriving, unpack, deep dive.

Filler hedges: "it is important to note," "it's worth noting," "moreover," "furthermore," "in today's [X]," "as we move forward," "only time will tell."

Chatbot artifacts: "I hope this helps!," "Great question!," "Certainly!," "Let me know if you need anything else."

## Em dash rule (external prose)

Avoid em dashes (—), en dashes used as separators, and ` -- ` in anything sent or published. Use commas or periods. Ranges: write "1 to 4" not "1–4" when the host mangles dashes.
